# Sensor Fusion — Game Spec

**Tagline:** *Four sensors. One car. Nobody sees everything.*
**Format:** Co-op, 2–5 players, ~8 minutes. Phones are controllers; a laptop/TV is the shared "dashboard" screen.
**Status:** design proposal from Claude for Codex to build against. Disagreements go in issue #1 (`[CODEX]`/`[CLAUDE]` protocol). Codex owns all implementation decisions.

**Provenance note:** items marked *design decision* are our choices, not verified mission requirements (the mission page is login-gated).

---

## 1. The one-sentence pitch

Each phone is one sensor on a self-driving car, so the team has to talk to each other to see the road — and if they brake for everything, they lose too.

## 2. What the player should feel

- **Second 10:** "Wait — I can only see dots? What do YOU see?"
- **Minute 1:** Shouting across the room: "It's tall and it's not moving!" "Map says that's a bridge!"
- **End:** "Radar saved us twice" — and they want to replay with roles swapped.

Every design choice below serves that arc. If a feature doesn't make players talk to each other, it's not a priority.

## 3. Roles

Each sensor has a real strength and a real blind spot. A sensor is **never** useful alone.

| Role | Shows | Blind spot | Visual identity (no art assets needed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Camera** | Color, traffic lights, signs, object *type* | Darkness, glare, heavy fog | Full-color top-down scene, heavy vignette; night/glare = dark or washed-out overlay |
| **Lidar** | Exact shape, size, distance | No color, no object type; degraded in heavy fog/rain | Cyan point cloud on black; size + distance readout on tap |
| **Radar** | Speed of moving objects (velocity arrows), works through fog | Low resolution (fuzzy blobs); weak on small/soft objects; stationary objects blend with background clutter | Green sweeping scope, blurry blips, arrows on movers |
| **Map** | Lanes, speed limit, known fixed structures (bridges, signals, crosswalks) | Can be **out of date** (no live objects at all) | Blueprint style: white lines on navy |
| **Driver** (shared screen) | The fused picture built from players' flags | Sees no raw sensor data | Clean dashboard, car, fused-object markers, controls |

**Player-count scaling:**
- 5 players: one per sensor + a Driver phone.
- 4 players: one per sensor; driving controls live on the shared screen, operated by whoever is closest, OR by majority tap (Codex: pick the simpler; log in DECISIONS.md).
- 3 players: Camera / Lidar+Map / Radar.
- 2 players: Camera+Radar / Lidar+Map.
- 1 player: Judge Mode (section 8).

## 4. Core loop

1. **Route start.** Car auto-drives a top-down 2D road. Team controls **speed** (and lane change in later routes).
2. **Hazard enters sensor range.** Each phone renders only its sensor's version of the scene, generated from one shared scenario definition (section 6).
3. **Flagging.** A player taps a blob in their view and picks a tag (e.g., `Object`, `Moving`, `Person?`, `Red light`, `Ignore`). Flags appear on the **Fusion Map** on the shared screen with the sensor's icon. Multiple sensors agreeing on the same object raise a visible **confidence ring**.
4. **Decide.** Brake, slow, proceed, (later) change lane. Slowing buys thinking time but drains the route clock.
5. **Resolve.** Collision, phantom brake, or clean pass.
6. **Debrief (8–12 s, skippable after 3 s).** Replay the moment with all four sensor layers stacked, then show the correct call side by side with the team's call, plus one or two sentences of "how a real AV handles this."

**The core tension:** being too cautious is also a failure. Hard braking for a harmless object (**phantom braking**, a real AV problem) costs points. Hitting a real hazard costs more. Braking every time loses.

## 5. Scoring

| Outcome | Points |
|---|---|
| Clean pass of a real hazard (correct stop/slow) | +100 |
| Clean pass through a harmless object (no brake) | +100 |
| Phantom brake (hard brake for harmless object) | −50 |
| Collision | −200 and car "limps" (slower) for the rest of the route |
| Arriving under the route's target time | +10 per second saved |
| Every flag that matched ground truth | +5 to that player's sensor score |

**End screen — "Team Perception Report"** (shareable card, screenshot-friendly):
- Final score and star rating (1–3)
- **MVP sensor** (most correct flags that mattered)
- A personality line based on behavior, e.g. *"You trusted Radar too much."* / *"Phantom brakers: your passengers have whiplash."* / *"Textbook fusion."* (Claude will write the full set of lines.)
- "Swap roles and replay" button

## 6. Scenarios (content)

**Design rule:** every sensor is the hero at least once and wrong (or useless) at least once.
Each scenario is **data**, not code: one definition object that every sensor view is rendered from.

| # | Scenario | Camera | Lidar | Radar | Map | Correct call |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Plastic bag drifting | Clearly a bag | Small low object | Barely/no return | Nothing | **Proceed** |
| 2 | Pedestrian, dark clothes, night | Nearly invisible | Clear human-sized shape | Faint slow mover | Crosswalk ahead | **Brake** |
| 3 | Stopped car under overpass | Car visible | Car-sized object at road level | Strong return, but it is stationary and merges with the bridge return | Overpass at that spot | **Brake** |
| 4 | Construction lane shift | Orange cones | Row of small objects | Weak/cluttered | **Shows lane open (map is out of date)** | **Slow + change lane** |
| 5 | Traffic light in sun glare | Washed out, color unreadable | Pole shape, no color | Nothing useful | Signal exists here | **Slow until readable** |
| 6 | Car cutting in from next lane | Car visible late | Car shape | **Fast lateral mover — first to show it** | Nothing | **Slow** |
| 7 | Ball rolling into road | Child's ball (implies child may follow) | Small round object | Weak return | School zone sign | **Slow / prepare to brake** |
| 8 | Heavy fog, stalled truck | Grey wall | Degraded, noisy points | **Clear stationary-ish return ahead** | Nothing | **Brake** |
| 9 | Overhead sign gantry | Sign visible above road | Structure above road height | Strong stationary return | Gantry at that spot | **Proceed** (radar alone would phantom-brake) |
| 10 | Deer at road edge, night | Eyes reflect, dim | Animal-sized shape at edge | Slow mover | Rural road | **Slow** |

**Routes:**
- **Route 1 "Suburb Day"** (tutorial-grade): scenarios 1, 6, 9
- **Route 2 "Downtown"**: 3, 4, 5, 7
- **Route 3 "Night Storm"**: 2, 8, 10 (+ camera lens gets "rain on the lens" mid-route, temporarily blurring the Camera view, forcing others to compensate)

**Fact-check status:** Radar difficulty with stationary objects and overhead structures (scenarios 3, 9) is well documented in automotive radar literature and patents (e.g., US7327855 on overhead structure detection; US12153159 on predicting radar false positives). Lidar degradation in fog and radar's relative robustness (scenario 8) is supported by published benchmarks (e.g., Bijelic et al., "A Benchmark for Lidar Sensors in Fog," arXiv:1912.03251; "Safe Autonomous Driving in Adverse Weather," arXiv:2305.01336). Debrief copy for each scenario will be written and checked by Claude before it ships.

## 7. Debrief copy (example tone)

> **Stopped car under an overpass.** Radar measures speed brilliantly, but a parked car and a bridge both look "stationary," so many radar systems discount still objects to avoid braking for every bridge. Lidar confirmed a car-sized object at road height, and the map showed the bridge sits overhead, not on the road. Fusing them makes it obvious: brake.

Short, plain, one idea. Claude writes all of these.

## 8. Judge Mode (*design decision*)

Not a stated mission requirement. Rationale: the rubric scores "demo-ready end-to-end," and a judge may test alone. Two options, both from the lobby:
1. **Drive with bot sensors:** bots flag objects with configurable accuracy ("Rookie sensors" ~70% correct, "Pro sensors" ~95%). The judge drives using only the Fusion Map — experiencing the game's central idea.
2. **Be one sensor:** judge picks a sensor; bots play the rest and a bot driver acts on flags. The judge sees how their flags change the outcome.

Also support opening each role in a separate browser tab on one computer (hot-seat testing).

## 9. Polish details that score points

- 20-second animated "how to play" on the shared screen, while the lobby fills
- Each sensor has a distinct sound (camera shutter tick, lidar chirp, radar ping, map paper rustle)
- Color-blind safety: never rely on color alone; Camera tags carry text labels too
- Portrait phone layout; big tap targets
- Reconnect: a dropped phone rejoins its same role with the same room code
- Late joiners take any open role between scenarios
- Vibration on Android when your sensor's flag mattered (iPhone Safari doesn't support it; optional)
- "Classroom mode" toggle: no timer, longer debriefs

## 10. First milestone scope (Codex's vertical slice in issue #1)

Proposed "one validated scenario": #3 (stopped car under overpass). Slice: lobby + room code, 2–4 roles, flagging to the Fusion Map, brake/proceed, resolve, a placeholder debrief. No sound, no art polish, no scoring beyond pass/fail.

## 11. Out of scope

Accounts, persistence, leaderboards across rooms, 3D, voice chat (players are in the same room). Live AI at runtime is excluded as a *design decision* (keeps facts accurate and outcomes deterministic), not a contest rule.

**Unsafe "correct action" assumption (Medium, applies to all "Proceed" answers).** The game must not teach "speed through an unidentified object." Real systems and good drivers slow down when uncertain. Fix in scoring:
- **Hard brake** for a harmless object = phantom-brake penalty (−50).
- **Gentle slow** = no penalty, only a small time cost.
- Only **confident, confirmed** proceed earns the full bonus.

This keeps the phantom-braking tension and stays honest about safety.

**Debrief wording rule.** Say "in this scenario, the fused evidence shows…" and never "an AV always does X." Real stacks are probabilistic and differ by company.
